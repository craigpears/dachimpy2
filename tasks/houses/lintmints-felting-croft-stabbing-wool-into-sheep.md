- 21 Nov: Real needle felting, what the puzzle is drawn from: a barbed needle stabbed into loose wool on a foam pad tangles the fibres; where you stab, the wool firms up and shrinks; more wool adds bulk; coloured wool laid on top and stabbed in makes the details; over-stab a spot and it goes hard and lumpy; stab crooked or pull sideways and the needle snaps.
- 21 Nov: Felting as a puzzle (first draft): the board a top view of a tuft of wool on the pad, a grid, the pattern’s outline faint on it. Each cell’s wool loose, felted, firm, or hard (over-stabbed, a lump). A stab (tap or click) on a cell: loose wool outside the outline is pulled one cell in toward the shape (its edge tucked in); inside the outline it firms a step; and the cells round it tighten a little too, so stabbing one spot again and again leaves a dimple with a lump, and even firming means spreading your stabs.
- 21 Nov: Its three phases, as real felting goes: the core (white wool, tuck everything outside the outline in), the shape (firm it all evenly, each cell changing colour as it firms, too many and it goes hard), the details (lay a coloured tuft, the sheep’s black face and legs or the cheese’s yellow rind, and stab it in; the cheese’s holes deep stabs). Three needles: a drag while stabbing, or stabbing too fast, bends then snaps one. A tap is a stab, so it plays naturally on a phone. Scored by how close to the pattern, how even, and needles left; the finished piece is yours to keep.
