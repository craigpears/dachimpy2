*A war frigate and a sloop on one board, and a blockade board shaped for both (13 Nov, for the weekend)*

The user, 13 Nov, last thing: "random big ideas for tomorrow ... I think we started on primates rule, but yeah, even
just like doing any kind of proof of concepts of what a big [war frig] on any kind of a board, like a navigation board,
would look like next to a sloop, and just how they would look moving around, interacting with each other, having some
kind of a blockade board whereby ... if you look at blockading, like where there's clusters that are ... clearly for
war frigates because they're out big and in the open, but then there's smaller coves that only ... [are] accessible to
small ships because of the narrower gaps. I think that would be a cool proof of concept to do over the weekend."

- **A war frigate on the sea board beside the sloop:** Primates Rule (src/den-frigate.ts, js/den/frigate.js; on the
  unlinked frigate-test.html, not yet at sea) sailing on the same square board as the sloop, at her size against the
  sloop's (src/ships.ts, `hullFor`), taking more than one square; how the two look moving round each other, passing,
  turning, side by side; a first look at them interacting (a broadside each, a frigate's bigger one).
- **A blockade board shaped by ship size:** like Puzzle Pirates' blockades, its open water in clusters out in the open,
  plainly frigate country, and small coves behind gaps too narrow for a frigate, a sloop's to hide in or hold. One
  board, both ships on it, the shape of the sea deciding which goes where. Ties in with "Bigger ships ride out the
  winds" (a deep-water mechanic) and Daquan's flotilla (the underdogs against the giant ship).
- A proof of concept: something to look at and steer, not a full game; on its own test page, like frigate-test.html.
- **Flags in the coves, a role for each ship (15 Nov):** the user, looking at Primates Rule: "I really want to be
  able to put this in [a] proverbial blockade." Little flags a sloop can take, set behind rocks in the small coves,
  so getting to one means running the gauntlet past the big ships: "if you try sailing past a sloop, past a war
  frigate in a sloop, you're probably going to get blown to pieces. But once you're past and you're in that little
  cove, you've got the control point." Some worth more, "really valuable 3-point flags", well worth sitting on. Taking
  one starts the fighting over it: the other side sends a sloop of its own, or a war frigate launches rowboats or
  smaller ships to contest it. "What can the little ships do that the big ships can't?" The answer gives every ship a
  role: "the points are captured by the sloops. The main areas are dominated by the big ships." The user knows it
  bends Puzzle Pirates' blockades ("somewhat against the spirit of blockades, but ... we're experimenting with
  concepts here"). (To shape with the user: how a flag's taken and held, what its points win, and whether a frigate
  launching boats is a real mechanic or a cutscene.)
- **Blasting through the gate (14 Nov):** the user, deciding the sloop race's imperial gate stays a choice
  (RACE.md §12): a gate everyone has to fight through, racing to shoot it down first, fits a bigger ship: "if
  you had a bigger ship with more guns ... you have to blast your way through the gate because that's the only
  way you can clear enough space for your big ship." A race or a run for frigates, the gate too narrow until
  it's blown open.
- **Racing in either (14 Nov):** pick a war frigate or a sloop for the sloop race, each opening routes of its own: the frigate battling through, the sloop nimble (RACE.md §11).
- **Primates Rule's own pictures (found 14 Nov):** Dachimpy.com's v3 on the user's NAS has PrimatesRule.jpg, her
  cabin and her galley (`//nas-pears/Projects/Dachimpy.com/Dachimpy.com/v3`), to check her look against.
