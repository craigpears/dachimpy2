after Helium's plan chased a Crystal Clear into a column of junk: "Is there no, again, like just method in the bot that sort of tries to predict and calculate what the odds are of actually getting all the whites into that column? Like if you've got a completely junk column, you don't try and build a whites into it. Yeah, you build further left. You start a new chain as far left as you can. Obviously, in the context of [CC^1], you don't need to. But like, if you're going for the maximalist one, absolutely." Today there's only the endgame's cash-in check (whites needed times the time a fix takes, against the time left); Helium's plan has a crude rule (more junk than white: start over). So a real estimate, a module of its own like the board reader: for the next column (and the ones after), the whites it needs, the whites near enough to bring in and how long that takes, against the time before it goes; the bots and Helium both deciding by it whether to carry the chain on, or let it go and start the next chain as far left as they can (the bigger the target, the further left), with tests on the user's board. Then (13 Nov), on Helium's rule (more junk than white in the next column: start over): "We want rules of thumb like that. That doesn't sound too far off how I would think. At least, I wouldn't make it more complicated unless we find some problems when we next review the list of example moves and situations." So no odds model: the rule of thumb stays, shared by the bots and Helium alike (one function), and is looked at again only if the next batch of positions shows a problem.
