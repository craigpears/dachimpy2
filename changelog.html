13 November

Helium's rum run

Fill a couple of casks at Helium's still and they'll ask you a favour: help get the rum where it's going. Say yes and the barrels are rolled aboard your sloop, her deck piled with them, and Helium hops on too.

Mischievious wants a wedding's worth on her jetty, and Kikkit gets a barrel (not a stash, Helium insists), either way round. Then the rest goes to Port Decorum, where the Empire loves rum but not where it comes from. Brazen it out at the jetty under the guard's nose, or slip round the back to the smugglers' beach. Sail anywhere near the Empire with rum aboard and Helium plays it all innocent, whistling, "nothing suspicious at all", while Dignity takes notes from her gate. Then take Helium home.

Try it: den.html?at=rumrun

Plan your race, then puzzle it through

You can't steer and puzzle at once, so now you don't have to. Down the right of the screen in the race is your plan: each leg still to come and your choice on it, the long way or one of its shortcuts. Click a leg to change it, on the starting line or halfway round, and your board stays live while you do.

The pilot sails your plan for you, move by move, and stops by itself wherever the course asks something of you: behind a headwind to hop, at a lock, the crane's ledge, the driftwood, the whirlpools' mouth, the starlit door. It puts up the board that place needs, and once you've done it she sails on. Throw a Double on the way in and she hops straight over without stopping. Want the helm back? Put in a move or click the sea, and the pilot carries on once she's sailed it; or turn the pilot off with T.

Your rivals race by plans too, and change them when a shortcut doesn't work out. Watching a race, you can see each racer's plan and every change as she makes it. Racing online, your plan stays yours: nobody else in the race sees it.

Try it: den.html?at=race; watch the plans: den.html?at=race-watch

12 November

Helium teaches Distilling

Helium has an island of their own now, out past the checkpoint near the smugglers' beach, with a whole distillery on it, like the ones in Puzzle Pirates: a timber building with a smoking chimney, a big copper still in the yard, a worm tub and its coil, a cask filling under the spout and a pile of full ones beside it. And a Distilling board on a stand. Sail up beside it and you see it happen: a column with a white in it goes down into the furnace, and the still blows up in their face: a flash, a fireball, smoke hanging about, copper and staves everywhere, its lid flying off and landing back on. "Let a white go down the furnace. Just a little distilling mishap." Is it safe? "Of course it's safe. Just don't send whites down the furnace."

Then they teach it, as if it were nothing, and slowly enough to follow. No basics (the game has its own tutorial for those), straight to technique, a step at a time. "First things first": every white in the three columns next to the fire lights up on the board, and they walk them out, swap by swap. "Otherwise, silly mishaps. Bang. And I've already got no eyebrows."

Then it's your go at their still, step one the goal, shown at the foot of the screen till you've done it. Do it and they'll tell you that's just a few of their tricks: the rest comes from other pirates, so ask about, watch them play, find their videos. Burn a white and the distillery blows up again. Send whites up and it gets to work: rum runs into the cask, and every full one is rolled onto the pile. Stuck? Press H, or the Help, Helium button: they look at your board, tell you what they'd do and why, name the technique, and do it for you, their words beside the board where you can read them, on a phone too. They'll also tell you about the big order of rum for Mischievious's next wedding, and Kikkit getting into their stash again.

Try it: den.html?at=helium

Race your friends online

Up to six of you on one course, the den's own racers filling any empty places. On the starting line press O, make a race and share its link, or type a friend's four-letter code. Everyone picks a spot, readies up, and anyone starts the count.

Each of you sails and puzzles your own sloop and sees everyone else's racing alongside. A lock or the crane is one racer's at a time, so you wait your turn in the berth. Broadsides hole your friends, and the places at the finish are the same for everyone. If someone's tab closes, a bot sails their sloop home. Every race is written down, so it can be watched again one day.

Try it: den.html?at=race, then O on the starting line

Make your own race course

The sloop race on a chart you can change. Click a buoy and drag it in to shorten a leg, or pick up a shortcut and slide it along its spit. Then set how big each puzzle is: how many Bilge clears raise you in the locks, how many pieces the crane takes to build, how many bundles of driftwood jam the barrier, and the gate guard's price.

A balance check races your course a few hundred times with the bots and shows how often each way round wins, and what each shortcut really saves. When you're happy, race it in 3D in the den (a link there brings you back), or copy a link to share it.

Try it: race-editor.html

The race's next pass: a bribe, the rocks, a fresh start every race, and a chart

The whole sloop race on one sheet of old parchment, drawn from the course itself: the long way round its five numbered buoys, and every shortcut through the spits, each labelled beside it with the puzzle it takes and the seconds it saves. The snakes are in red, the winds in blue, and the starlit door's tunnel runs dotted in purple. Hatched green are the sheltered spots, where even the leader can stop and puzzle without being shot. Beside each way round is how often it wins, from thousands of races sailed by bots: every way wins between about one race in seven and two in five, against a fair one in four. So pick the shortcuts that are your puzzles.

Every shortcut in proportion. Each stopped puzzle (the gate, the barrier, the locks, the crane, the rowing gap, the rocks) now cuts about the same stretch of course, around twenty seconds, and takes about as long to play, so the only question is which puzzle is yours. The hops grow with their chain: a Double a little, a Triple more, a Vegas most.

A bribe at the gate. The gun gap on the first spit is an imperial gate, with a gatehouse, the Empire's banners and a guard who can be bought. Back along the first leg an old wreck lies aground in a sheltered notch: haul up 40 coins by Treasure Haul, hand them over, and his guns hold their fire while his men tow you through. Or rush it on a big combo, or shoot its guns quiet.

Rocks to blast open, for everyone. Off the third leg a wall of boulders blocks a gap. Three broadside hits bring it down, anyone's, as you sail by, and once it's down the gap is open to every racer for the rest of the race. The leader won't want to open it for the pack; someone at the back might.

A fresh start every race. Below the starting line, a field of slow winds and whirlpools laid fresh each race (online, the same for everyone): a slow wind nudges you one square after each move, a whirlpool turns you round to its far corner, so you eyeball your way through. Or hop its headwinds with a Double for a ride down toward the first buoy.

Gentler snakes. A wind that catches you now carries you back just a few squares, to have another go at what you were doing, never back to the start.

More on the thinking behind it in How the Pirate Den is designed.

Try it: den.html?at=race

Watch the race

Sit back and watch four racers go round with nobody else in it: Kikkit, Tzz, Dalnoth with Umami aboard, and Gravee, who takes the locks. Fly the camera anywhere over the course, or press 1 to 4 to follow a racer, Tab for the next. A bar along the top says where she's placed, which leg she's on and what she's up to.

Follow one and you see her puzzling, move by move: her Sailing board as she sails and builds her Vegas, Gravee's Bilge on the lock's quay, Umami's Foraging on the driftwood, Dalnoth loading his guns, and a patch on her deck when she's hit.

Try it: den.html?at=race-watch

11 November

The sloop race

Out past Primates Rule, race Kikkit, Tzz and Dalnoth round a zigzag course: pick your spot on the starting line past a field of whirlpools and winds, then five legs back and forth round numbered buoys to a proper finish line. Every bend has a long way round that needs no puzzle, and a shortcut through the spit that needs one: a gap guarded by guns (a heavy combo zooms you past, or knock them out by Gunnery), a headwind you row through, whirlpools only a Vegas clears, two locks pumped by Bilge, a crane you build by Carpentry, and a barrier of driftwood cleared by Foraging. Each one saves about what it takes to play, so take the ones that are your puzzles.

Each rival has her own sloop, colours and way round: Kikkit goes all out for a Vegas over the whirlpools, Tzz hops the little gaps with Doubles and never fires a shot, and Dalnoth takes the driftwood with Umami the capybara aboard to forage. Sit still out in the open and they'll shoot you, and you can shoot back with Q and E; a hit leaves a sloop smoking until it's patched. The locks, the crane's ledge and the barrier are sheltered. Watch for the snakes, trade winds that sweep you a long way back (a well-timed Double hops them). Your place shows as you race, everyone's places and times at the finish, and your best time's kept. The course was tuned over thousands of simulated races so every route has a fair chance.

Try it: den.html?at=race

The starlit door

A shortcut for Navigation players, built into the sloop race. Off the second leg there's a door in the cliff, stars round it. Stop under it, press Space, and Duty Navigation stands on the rock as the night sky. Fill its constellation and its stars light on the carved wheel above, and the door swings open on a tunnel, long and dark.

Through it goes your sloop, along the cavern under the spit and up a cleft in the rock onto the third leg, about 40 seconds ahead and back among the racers. Navigation is the whole of it: no combo to get in, nothing to choose in the dark.

Try it: den.html?at=door

Duty Navigation

Puzzle Pirates' Duty Navigation, drawn as the real thing: an upright chart of the night sky, three rings to turn, stars dropped in to fall towards the middle, lines of three to clear, and constellations to fill before you booch.

Try it: navigation-test.html

Port Decorum, the imperial island

Out past the imperial checkpoint, the empire's own port: a fort whose walls tower over your sloop's mast, its keep the tallest thing on the sea, two-storey town buildings along the quay, and a jetty to tie up at. A first sketch of a place to sail to.

The smugglers' beach. Round the back of the island, behind a wall of rocks, a narrow run of water leads down to a cove and a little sandy beach under the fort, with barrels and a rowboat drawn up on it. The only way in is from the penguins' water past Kikkit's whirlpools. Nothing doing there yet: it's where smuggling will come in.

Try it: den.html?at=imperial, or the beach, den.html?at=smugglers

Tzz's balloon lesson

A hop from Kikkit's island, Tzz blows up balloons and plays them onto a real Sailing board beside her, showing you how a chain's really built, step by step: the gap left as the trigger, the stack of three, the balloon that drops into the gap, all marked on the board. Then it's your go.

Try it: den.html?at=tzz

Gravee's house: Bedside Manors

Knock at the houses in the den's cliff. The first is Gravee's. She walks you round her portraits and talks about each one you look at, the vampire sits up in his coffin, and she paints you scared to death for her wall.

Try it: den.html?at=lodgings, then Gravee's house

Kikkit's whirlpools

Kikkit on her little island beside the reef, explaining whirlpools (taken literally) by Puzzle Pirates' rules, with plenty of tangents. Sail through the reef by them, and with a Vegas reach the penguins on Vegas Island beyond.

Vegas Island. Only a Vegas over all of the whirlpools gets you there: a little ice floe ringed by rocks, and a colony of penguins pottering about on it. One of them's in a balaclava with a sack, up to something, and freezes when you turn up.

Try it: den.html?at=kikkit, or the penguins, den.html?at=penguins

Mischievious's wedding

Umami's rescue

A crane you build by Carpentry hauls the sloop up the cove's cliff into a still, reedy lagoon. On its far bank Umami the capybara is shut in an old fish trap. Free him by Foraging and he comes home to live in the den's shallows.

The imperial checkpoint, and Dignity

Its guns are trained on marks in the water, so getting past takes some work. You can run it, row it, knock its guns out, or find the secret way round by the locks. The first time you sail up, its commander Dignity peers down from the gate, and her monkey Bella fires the warning shot.

Try it: den.html?at=dignity

Rowing

Row the sloop by port and starboard: two boards of mirror strokes, one for each side's oars. The turn you're building shows on her: the leading side's oars sweep harder, and her bows swing and heel toward it (the compass in this first shot has since gone). It's the way through calms and headwinds, and through the checkpoint's guns.

Bilging the shallows, and powder barrels

The real Bilge board lies on the beach, and its water is the tide. Good moves draw the tide back so bare rocks cut off the Vikings rowing in.
